# sql-sakila

## Business Question
Which store-category combinations have the highest rentals per copy (24 May to 31 Aug 2005), and where might the manager add copies?

## Approach
**Scope:** I used rentals from 24th May to 31st August 2005 because it is a continuous period, excluding the missing subsequent months and the last partial month. May is partial but included because the comparison is within the same window. I also used count(distinct inventory.inventory_id) in the query to ensure that duplicates are not included in the analysis.

**Data check:** The date range showed 24th May 2005 to 14th February 2006. However, the first and last months are partial, while the months September 2005 to January 2006 do not exist in the dataset. I excluded February 2006 because of the missing data from prior months, which could potentially skew the data. I also checked and confirmed that each film belongs to only one category, so joins don’t duplicate rows.

**Store attribution:** I used inventory.store_id because the decision is about shelf stock. I tested this by whether inventory.store_id and customer.store_id match, and found 50% of rentals mismatch, indicating that about 50% of rentals occur at a store other than the customer's home store. Therefore, using customer.store_id might change the results.

**Measure:** rentals per copy per store and category, 24 May to 31 August 2005, store from inventory.store_id, idle copies kept (4581 copies). 42 films have no copies and can't appear in any rental-based result. 

SQL queries are in [queries.sql](https://github.com/nasir-ay/sql-sakila/blob/main/queries.sql)

## Key Findings
1. The gap between the best and worst store-category combination based on rentals per copy (Documentary vs Horror, store 2: 3.65 vs 3.32) is about 9%, similar in size to the variation expected by chance for counts of 450 to 600 rentals (roughly 6%, though not formally tested), so I can’t conclude any category has genuinely higher demand per copy.
2. Rentals per copy is close to even, partly because stock is already roughly proportional to demand (Documentary store 2 has 164 copies and 599 rentals, while Horror has 136 copies and 451 rentals).


## Recommendations
The manager needn't add copies to any categories right now. Documentary in store 2 has the highest rentals per copy, but a 9% gap over three full months and eight days is small. 
Rentals per copy is close to even partly because stock is already roughly proportional to demand (Documentary store 2 has 164 copies and 599 rentals; Horror has 136 and 451). Also, rentals per copy shows demand per copy, not whether stock is adequate, and evenly used copies could all be over-stocked or under-stocked. Additional data, like rental duration, may yield a stronger recommendation.

## Next steps
Rental duration, which converts rentals per copy into a utilisation rate, was not calculated, although rental date and return date were included. The average duration of rentals should be calculated to determine which copies sit idle most of the time.
The amount of revenue generated by each rental should be evaluated using amount from the payment table. With this analysis, a category that generates high revenue per rental may deserve more copies than the rental count suggests.
